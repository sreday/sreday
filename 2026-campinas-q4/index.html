IBM

Luiz Siqueira & Maria Luiza de Queiroz

SRE as a Service - Acelerando a adoção SRE

Bradesco
Como transformar princípios de SRE em uma jornada prática, priorizada e incorporada ao fluxo real dos times? Nesta palestra, apresentaremos o SRE as a Service, plataforma que centraliza e acelera a adoção de práticas de confiabilidade. A iniciativa transforma a pirâmide de SRE em um modelo operacional: cada camada se desdobra em práticas estruturadas, e cada prática se converte em itens do backlog das squads. A plataforma conecta práticas como SLO, Error Budget, Correlation Deployment, Chaos Engineering e Post-mortem a uma jornada de evolução. Cada prática detalha desde o objetivo até a atividade que a squad executa, com premissas, critérios de entrada, aceite e conclusão, a evidência esperada e os indicadores corporativos que ela impulsiona. É o que reduz a distância entre uma recomendação de SRE e a sua implementação. Mostraremos a avaliação de maturidade, que é o elemento central dessa jornada. Ela fornece à squad um parâmetro objetivo sobre seu estágio em cada prática, reduzindo a subjetividade e permitindo comparações ao longo da jornada e entre diferentes squads. Como esse processo é contínuo, a maturidade passa a ser acompanhada como um indicador de evolução do time. A jornada não se limita ao diagnóstico: os resultados da avaliação identificam oportunidades de melhoria e orientam a priorização de ações para elevar a maturidade. Essas ações são estruturadas em backlog e integradas ao Jira, facilitando seu acompanhamento e a evolução da squad. Como exemplo concreto, detalharemos a prática de definição de SLOs: do mapeamento da jornada do usuário e dos SLIs ao contrato de SLO, Error Budget e dashboards de acompanhamento. Também discutiremos como relacionar deploys a sinais operacionais, testar cenários de falha de forma controlada e usar os aprendizados para evoluir continuamente o serviço. A principal lição é que escalar SRE não significa somente escalar ferramentas ou criar mais padrões. Significa criar um produto interno que traduza confiabilidade em decisões, aprendizado e trabalho priorizado para os times. audience: - "SREs" - "Platform Engineers" - "DevOps Engineers" - "Lideranças de Engenharia" - "Gestores de Confiabilidade" - "Profissionais que buscam escalar a adoção de SRE em múltiplos times" topics: - "Adoção de SRE" - "Cultura e formas de trabalho" - "SLOs, SLIs e Error Budgets" - "Maturidade de confiabilidade" - "Backlog centralizado" - "Integração com Jira" - "Plataforma interna" presentation_structure: - "O desafio de escalar a adoção de SRE." - "A pirâmide de SRE como estrutura para organizar práticas." - "Da prática ao backlog integrado ao Jira." - "Mapa mental de práticas e jornada de adoção." - "Exemplo prático: definição de SLOs." - "Avaliação de maturidade por jornada e squad." - "Lições aprendidas e próximos passos."... WorkshopKill It and Watch It Come Back: Hands-on with Reliaburger

3M
Before your first container hits production on Kubernetes you install a distro, a CNI, an ingress, cert-manager, Prometheus, Loki and ArgoCD. None of them is your app. After ten years of running platform teams on Kubernetes, I built Reliaburger to put the lot into one Rust binary: scheduler, gossip, Raft, eBPF service discovery, ingress, mTLS, registry, metrics, logs, GitOps and chaos testing, driven by TOML. An app with health checks, ingress and autoscaling is twelve lines. This is a hands-on session, so bring a laptop (macOS on Apple silicon or Linux; Windows is not supported yet). You will: Install Reliaburger with one command and start a node on your laptop. No container runtime, no root, no VMs: the built-in process runtime runs plain binaries. Deploy an app from a twelve-line TOML file and watch it in the terminal dashboard and the web UI. Ship a deliberately sick version and watch the health checker restart it, then roll back with one command. Break it on purpose with the built-in fault injector: kill an instance, freeze another, and let relish wtf explain what just happened. Read the manual and the source code from inside the binary. I will be next to you for the questions the manual does not answer. Provide feedback! It is 0.2.0. It will break. That is the point, and I want to hear about it.... Read more

Marcelo Capile

Diagnose, Approve, Remediate, Verify: A Two-Agent AIOps Loop for Kubernetes

IBM
Kubernetes incidents usually start the same way: an alert fires, someone checks events, runs a few describe and logs commands, builds a hypothesis, and decides whether it's safe to take action. Although this process is familiar to most SREs, it is still largely manual, time-consuming, and often difficult to document consistently. In our team, we started asking a simple question: how much of that investigation could be automated while still keeping humans in control of critical decisions? This talk presents a two-agent AIOps architecture designed around that idea. A collector monitors Kubernetes Warning events and triggers a read-only diagnostic agent. Through Kubernetes tools exposed by MCP, the agent gathers evidence, investigates affected resources, analyzes possible causes, and generates a structured incident report that an operator can review. The key design decision was to separate diagnosis from remediation. The diagnostic agent can investigate and suggest actions, but it cannot make changes. The operator remains responsible for deciding whether a proposed remediation is appropriate. Only after explicit approval does a second agent perform the corrective action. Every step is tracked. The system validates the cluster state after remediation, records the outcome, and preserves reports, decisions, failures, and state transitions to provide a complete audit trail.... Read more

WorkshopKill It and Watch It Come Back: Hands-on with Reliaburger
Abstract

Before your first container hits production on Kubernetes you install a distro, a CNI, an ingress, cert-manager, Prometheus, Loki and ArgoCD. None of them is your app. After ten years of running platform teams on Kubernetes, I built Reliaburger to put the lot into one Rust binary: scheduler, gossip, Raft, eBPF service discovery, ingress, mTLS, registry, metrics, logs, GitOps and chaos testing, driven by TOML.   3. Ship a deliberately sick version and watch the health checker restart it, then roll back with one command.
  4. Break it on purpose with the built-in fault injector: kill an instance, freeze another, and let relish wtf explain what just happened.
  5. Read the manual and the source code from inside the binary. I will be next to you for the questions the manual does not answer.

Diagnose, Approve, Remediate, Verify: A Two-Agent AIOps Loop for Kubernetes
Abstract

Kubernetes incidents usually start the same way: an alert fires, someone checks events, runs a few describe and logs commands, builds a hypothesis, and decides whether it's safe to take action. Although this process is familiar to most SREs, it is still largely manual, time-consuming, and often difficult to document consistently. In our team, we started asking a simple question: how much of that investigation could be automated while still keeping humans in control of critical decisions? This talk presents a two-agent AIOps architecture designed around that idea. A collector monitors Kubernetes Warning events and triggers a read-only diagnostic agent. Through Kubernetes tools exposed by MCP, the agent gathers evidence, investigates affected resources, analyzes possible causes, and generates a structured incident report that an operator can review. The key design decision was to separate diagnosis from remediation. The diagnostic agent can investigate and suggest actions, but it cannot make changes. The operator remains responsible for deciding whether a proposed remediation is appropriate. Only after explicit approval does a second agent perform the corrective action. Every step is tracked. The system validates the cluster state after remediation, records the outcome, and preserves reports, decisions, failures, and state transitions to provide a complete audit trail.
